## Why I tried it

My workflow used to look like this: one subscription for Kling, another for image models, a third for whatever went viral that week, and a spreadsheet to track which credits expired when. Every new model meant a new login and a new pricing page to decode.

I wanted three things: every top model in one place, a price I could see before I hit generate, and a way to keep the same character across a week of posts. SocialSight was the first platform I tried that checked all three.

## Character Factory

Consistent characters used to be the hardest part of my job. Character Factory made it a five-minute task.

- **Start anywhere.** Pick traits, upload up to five source photos, or remix one of 21 full-body character blueprints.
- **Pick from four looks.** It generates four candidate looks. I refine my favorite and save it with its own handle.
- **Cast it in anything.** I mention the handle in any prompt, or hit **Cast in Video** and the generator opens with my character already in the shot.
- **Give it a voice.** I assign a voice from my voice library, including recorded and cloned voices, so the character sounds the same in every clip.
- **Ten free.** Every plan includes 10 free character generations. After that, a character costs 50 tokens.

If you run a faceless channel or an AI influencer account, this alone is worth the switch. [Build your first character free](https://socialsight.ai/?utm_source=github).

## The content creation agent

This is the feature I didn't expect to like. I describe the video I want, and the agent handles the rest:

1. **It reads my goal.** I tell it what the video is for, or start from a template with one or two blanks.
2. **It plans the shots.** It splits my idea into short, consistent shots, which makes sense, because AI video holds quality better in short takes.
3. **It holds my references.** My character, product, and style carry across every shot.
4. **It shows me the storyboard and the cost.** I see keyframes and the exact token cost before anything renders.
5. **It assembles the video.** The shots come back as one finished video, saved to my project.

Here's a brief I ran:

```
A 30 second ad for a cold brew coffee brand aimed at college students. Open on
a 2am study session, cut to the can cracking open, end on a sunrise walk to
class. Upbeat, funny, 9:16. Use my character @mila.
```

For brand work, Marketing Studio turns a product and a brief into coordinated image and video concepts from a library of templates. And because SocialSight has an MCP server, I can run all of it from Claude, ChatGPT, or Cursor. [Try the agent on SocialSight](https://socialsight.ai/?utm_source=github).

## Editing clips I already have

Half my work is fixing footage, not generating it. SocialSight handles that too:

- **Edit with a prompt.** Seedance 2.5's Edit mode takes a 4 to 30 second clip and rewrites it to my instructions.
- **Extend any clip.** Seedance 2.5 and Gemini Omni Flash 1.1 continue a video forward, no prompt required.
- **Direct with keyframes.** Kling 4.0 edits an input video and follows an ordered strip of up to 8 middle frames.
- **Change the voice.** The voice changer takes audio or video uploads up to 200 MB.
- **Finish it.** Upscale images and video, trim clips, and send any image to the conversational Image Editor agent with **Edit with agent**.

## What I didn't love

No platform is perfect. A few things to know:

- **The top video models need a paid plan.** Seedance, Wan 3.0, and Kling 4.0 aren't on the free tier.
- **43 models is a lot.** On day one I wasn't sure which to pick. The model guides help, and the prompts above are a good starting point.
- **Pure film work.** If you're cutting a short film with lens and era controls, Higgsfield's Cinema Studio goes deeper on camera direction.
